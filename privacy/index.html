The short version.

The free planner and the website

Your plan stays on your device

The free planner has no account. Your blocks, categories and templates are saved in your browser’s storage on your device. Backup files and calendar (.ics) files are made on your device and go wherever you save them. We never receive them. If you clear your browser’s data, your plan is gone unless you made a backup.

A downloaded copy of the planner runs on your computer with no analytics at all.

Analytics

On daymarkweekly.app, we use two analytics services to see how people find and use the site:

  • Google Analytics records pages viewed, scrolling, outbound clicks, video plays and file downloads, with your approximate location, device and browser, and the site you came from.
  • Microsoft Clarity records how pages are used, such as clicks, taps, scrolling and mouse movement, to show heatmaps and replays of visits. Everything you type in the planner is masked before it leaves your browser, including blocks, categories, templates and dialogs, so Clarity never sees what your plan says.

Both set cookies or similar identifiers to recognise a returning browser, and both see your IP address. We don’t send them your name, email or anything from your plan. You can block them with your browser’s tracking protection or a content blocker, or with Google’s opt-out add-on. The planner works the same either way.

Hosting and fonts

The site is hosted by GitHub Pages and loads its font from Google Fonts. Both receive your IP address and browser details when they deliver files, as any web server does, and may keep them in their logs.

Where your data is processed

We’re in Canada, and most of our providers store and process data in the United States, where it is protected by their contracts with us but also subject to US law. For people in the European Union, the UK and Switzerland, transfers rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions where a provider is certified, and otherwise on the standard contractual clauses in the provider’s data processing terms.

Legal bases

If you’re in the EU or the UK, we rely on these legal bases:

  • Contract: your account, plans, log, sync, subscription and support.
  • Consent: AI features, notes sent to AI, reminders, usage counts and the microphone. You can withdraw consent at any time in settings.
  • Legitimate interests: website analytics, security, and preventing abuse and fraud.
  • Legal obligation: tax and accounting records.

In Canada, we rely on your consent, which you give by using the services as this policy describes, and which you can withdraw subject to legal and contractual limits.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and everything in it: until you delete it. Delete my account in the Pro planner’s account menu, or Delete everything in Time audit’s settings, cancels any Pro subscription, then deletes your plans, log, settings and account at once. Copies can remain in our providers’ backups for a short time, until those are overwritten.
  • Stripe webhook records (ids, types and times only): 30 days.
  • Payment records at Stripe and Link: as long as tax and financial law requires, usually 6 to 10 years.
  • Support emails: up to 2 years after the conversation ends.
  • Website analytics: Google Analytics keeps event data for up to 14 months. Clarity keeps recordings for about 30 days and heatmaps for up to 13 months.

Your rights

You can ask to see the personal information we hold about you, to correct it, to get a copy in a portable format, to delete it, or to withdraw a consent. You can do most of this yourself:

  • Export: the Pro planner’s account menu saves a Backup file that the free planner opens, and Time audit exports your log as a CSV file.
  • Delete: Delete my account in the Pro planner’s account menu, or Delete everything in Time audit’s settings.
  • Correct: edit anything in the app, and your email in your account settings.

For anything else, write to support@daymarkweekly.app. We’ll answer within 30 days, and may need to confirm it’s really you first. There’s no charge.

If you’re in the EU or the UK, you can also object to processing based on legitimate interests and ask us to restrict processing. If you’re in a US state with a privacy law, such as California, you have the rights to know, delete, correct and opt out; we don’t sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising, so there’s nothing to opt out of, and we won’t treat you differently for using your rights.

If you’re not happy with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, Quebec’s Commission d’accès à l’information if you live in Quebec, or your local data protection authority in the EU or the UK.

Security

Data travels encrypted (HTTPS) and is encrypted where our providers store it. Only you can read your account’s data through the app; every request is checked against your sign-in on the server. We look at an account’s data only when its owner asks for help, or when the law requires it. If a breach puts your personal information at risk, we’ll tell you and the authorities the law requires, as quickly as we can.

Children

The services aren’t meant for children. You must be at least 16 to create an account. If you think a child has given us personal information, write to us and we’ll delete it.

Changes to this policy

When we change this policy, we’ll update the date at the top. If a change matters, such as a new kind of data or a new provider that sees your plans, we’ll tell account holders by email or in the app before it applies.
